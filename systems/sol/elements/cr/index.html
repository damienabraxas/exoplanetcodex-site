How to read this forest. Each row is one independent product, preserving its band, holding, engine, indicator and line set. Engines and indicator families are never averaged. Each bar is the uncertainty as Asplund et al. 2021 quote it: line-to-line scatter, the 3D-model term and the NLTE term (at least 0.03 dex). For a molecule the NLTE term is replaced by its dependence on the other element, and the 3D term comes from the 3D-to-1D difference Amarsi et al. 2021 publish for the same band. Atomic-data and observational terms are not in the bar, as in Asplund; they stay in the full error budget attached to the row. Grades describe the supplied line-pool provenance, not engine quality; evaluated theoretical CNO oscillator strengths are not relabelled as laboratory evidence. How this was measured

Every chromium value on this page comes from fitting synthetic spectra to the observed solar spectrum, line by line or band by band, with the chromium abundance as the free parameter. The synthesis includes the atomic lines and the molecular opacity in every window, so blended absorption is modelled rather than attributed to the line being measured. The continuum is placed locally around every line by comparing the observation with the synthesis on its own highest pixels, and ground-based spectra are telluric-corrected across their full range.

Each indicator is measured independently on each solar spectrum and reported separately; nothing is averaged across indicators or instruments. Where a 3D or NLTE correction is published for the lines, it is applied as its own product (1D-LTE, 1D-NLTE and 3D-NLTE are separate rows). Every value carries a full uncertainty budget: fit, line strengths, microturbulence, continuum, fit window, model atmosphere, telluric residual, and the spread between spectra, each measured by refitting with that one input changed. The headline at the top is the Asplund-grade product: a published line set, the most complete treatment, then the most lines.

Solar chromium context

Chromium is measured from neutral Cr I and ionized Cr II lines from the near-ultraviolet to the near-infrared. The reference lines are the Cr I and Cr II lines Asplund et al. 2021 used, with the oscillator strengths they adopted; a wider set of lines with laboratory oscillator strengths and the Codex's own line selection are shown as separate products. The visible lines agree with Asplund. The near-infrared Cr I lines all come from one multiplet that the literature does not use, and they read about 0.5 dex high on every spectrum. The most likely cause is the laboratory oscillator strengths for this multiplet. Lines from the same upper levels and from the same lower level read normally in the blue and visible, and iron lines in the same infrared window read normally, so the spectrum and the model atmosphere are not to blame. The offset is the same for weak and strong lines, so it is not a blend or line broadening. Sobeck et al. 2007 calibrated their infrared values with a separate check on exactly these lines and did not use them on the Sun. We show the products as measured until better laboratory values exist. The near-ultraviolet result depends on which version of the Kitt Peak atlas is used.
