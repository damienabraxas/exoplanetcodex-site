How this was measured

Every nickel value on this page comes from fitting synthetic spectra to the observed solar spectrum, line by line or band by band, with the nickel abundance as the free parameter. The synthesis includes the atomic lines and the molecular opacity in every window, so blended absorption is modelled rather than attributed to the line being measured. The continuum is placed locally around every line by comparing the observation with the synthesis on its own highest pixels, and ground-based spectra are telluric-corrected across their full range.

Each indicator is measured independently on each solar spectrum and reported separately; nothing is averaged across indicators or instruments. Where a 3D or NLTE correction is published for the lines, it is applied as its own product (1D-LTE, 1D-NLTE and 3D-NLTE are separate rows). Every value carries a full uncertainty budget: fit, line strengths, microturbulence, continuum, fit window, model atmosphere, telluric residual, and the spread between spectra, each measured by refitting with that one input changed. The headline at the top is the Asplund-grade product: a published line set, the most complete treatment, then the most lines.

Solar nickel context

Solar nickel is measured from neutral Ni I lines and compared with ionized Ni II. The optical Ni I products include laboratory oscillator strengths. The infrared Ni I set uses the lines Elgueta et al. 2026 identified as reliable for Sun-like stars and calculated oscillator strengths. The Ni II set uses calculated oscillator strengths from Clear et al. 2026. Both sets are shown as Theoretical Codex. The H-band result is sensitive to continuum placement. The nickel model atom supports non-LTE line formation for Ni I.
