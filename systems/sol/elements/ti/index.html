How this was measured

Every titanium value on this page comes from fitting synthetic spectra to the observed solar spectrum, line by line or band by band, with the titanium abundance as the free parameter. The synthesis includes the atomic lines and the molecular opacity in every window, so blended absorption is modelled rather than attributed to the line being measured. The continuum is placed locally around every line by comparing the observation with the synthesis on its own highest pixels, and ground-based spectra are telluric-corrected across their full range.

Each indicator is measured independently on each solar spectrum and reported separately; nothing is averaged across indicators or instruments. Where a 3D or NLTE correction is published for the lines, it is applied as its own product (1D-LTE, 1D-NLTE and 3D-NLTE are separate rows). Every value carries a full uncertainty budget: fit, line strengths, microturbulence, continuum, fit window, model atmosphere, telluric residual, and the spread between spectra, each measured by refitting with that one input changed. The headline at the top is the Asplund-grade product: a published line set, the most complete treatment, then the most lines.

Solar titanium context

Titanium is measured from neutral Ti I and ionized Ti II lines from the near-ultraviolet to the near-infrared. The reference lines are the Ti I and Ti II lines Asplund et al. 2021 used, with the oscillator strengths they adopted; a wider set of lines with laboratory oscillator strengths and the Codex's own line selection are shown as separate products. Ti I departs from LTE, so each spectrum also carries non-LTE products. No titanium line in the H or K band has a published oscillator-strength uncertainty, so those bands are gaps.
