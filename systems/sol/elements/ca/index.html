Calcium Appendix

Ca · products, publication readiness and reproducible evidence

Solar calcium: 6.405 ± 0.049

Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse 2021: 6.30 ± 0.03  ·  this measurement +0.105 dex

The Ca I value sits about 0.1 dex above Asplund et al. 2021. They fit the light from the centre of the solar disc; we fit the light of the whole disc, as we must for other stars. Fitting the disc centre with the same lines and code gives their value, so the difference comes from the strong lines' edge-of-disc light, not from the line data. The Ca II lines agree with Asplund et al. 2021. The infrared values read about 0.1 dex higher still. On iron lines that also have laboratory values, the Cepheid-calibrated oscillator strengths sit about 0.14 dex low, which would push a calcium abundance up by about that much; the infrared products are shown as measured, with that caveat.

Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse (2021) give no line recipe we can repeat for this element, so this is our best HARPS product: the Reference Grade analysis with the most complete treatment, then the most lines.

How to read this forest. Each row is one independent product, preserving its band, holding, engine, indicator and line set. Engines and indicator families are never averaged. Each bar is the uncertainty as Asplund et al. 2021 quote it: line-to-line scatter, the 3D-model term and the NLTE term (at least 0.03 dex). For a molecule the NLTE term is replaced by its dependence on the other element, and the 3D term comes from the 3D-to-1D difference Amarsi et al. 2021 publish for the same band. Atomic-data and observational terms are not in the bar, as in Asplund; they stay in the full error budget attached to the row. Grades describe the supplied line-pool provenance, not engine quality; evaluated theoretical CNO oscillator strengths are not relabelled as laboratory evidence. Quarantined, failed and unconstrained fits have no abundance mark. Experimental products remain explicitly not adopted. Literature references provide context, never tuning targets.

How this was measured

Every calcium value on this page comes from fitting synthetic spectra to the observed solar spectrum, line by line or band by band, with the calcium abundance as the free parameter. The synthesis includes the atomic lines and the molecular opacity in every window, so blended absorption is modelled rather than attributed to the line being measured. The continuum is placed locally around every line by comparing the observation with the synthesis on its own highest pixels, and ground-based spectra are telluric-corrected across their full range.

Each indicator is measured independently on each solar spectrum and reported separately; nothing is averaged across indicators or instruments. Where a 3D or NLTE correction is published for the lines, it is applied as its own product (1D-LTE, 1D-NLTE and 3D-NLTE are separate rows). Every value carries a full uncertainty budget: fit, line strengths, microturbulence, continuum, fit window, model atmosphere, telluric residual, and the spread between spectra, each measured by refitting with that one input changed.     Solar calcium context

    Calcium is measured from neutral Ca I and ionized Ca II lines in the visible and red-optical. The reference lines are the ten Ca I and five Ca II lines Asplund et al. 2021 used, with the oscillator strengths they adopted; a wider set of lines with laboratory oscillator strengths and the Codex's own line selection are shown as separate products. In the infrared (Y and J bands, Kitt Peak and CRIRES+) no laboratory oscillator strength exists, so the lines use values Elgueta et al. 2024 calibrated on Cepheid stars, each with its own published uncertainty. No usable calcium line was found in the near-ultraviolet, and none in the H and K bands has an oscillator strength that was not itself tuned to the Sun.
