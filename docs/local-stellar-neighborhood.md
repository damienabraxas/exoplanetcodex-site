# Local stellar-neighborhood map

The homepage map is a public scientific data visualization generated by RYA-1065. Its versioned payload is `assets/data/local-stellar-neighborhood.v1.json`; no target distance or coordinate is declared in the page or renderer.

## Coordinates and provenance

Target membership, identifiers, roles, spectral types, and slugs come from every row of the science repository's `data/catalog/system_catalog.csv`. `config/stars.yaml` / `STAR_PARAMS` is an optional physics join, never a membership filter, so a registered future target remains visible before its fundamental parameters are adopted. Fixed-star ICRS J2000 positions, positive SIMBAD parallaxes, and bibliography codes live in `data/reference/local_neighborhood_astrometry.csv`. Distance is the direct inverse parallax, `distance_pc = 1000 / parallax_mas`.

The generator uses Astropy `SkyCoord` to transform ICRS position plus distance into heliocentric Galactic Cartesian coordinates. `x_gc` points toward the Galactic center, `y_rotation` toward Galactic longitude 90 degrees (the direction of Galactic rotation), and `z_ngp` toward the north Galactic pole. The homepage renders the orthographic `x_gc`–`y_rotation` projection with equal scale on both axes. Scientific coordinates are not offset to separate labels or markers; Alpha Centauri A and B therefore appear nearly coincident at neighborhood scale.

The Sun is the coordinate origin and has no fixed sky position. A target URL is emitted only when the catalog slug resolves to an actual site `index.html`; unpublished targets remain focusable for their name and distance but receive no invented link.

## Regeneration

From the science repository:

```bash
python3 scripts/generate_local_stellar_neighborhood.py \
  --site-root /path/to/exoplanetcodex-site
```

The generator validates schema compatibility, provenance, duplicate IDs/slugs, finite positive distances, coordinate ranges, Cartesian finiteness, and every published route before writing both the canonical science product and the site copy. The visualization uses native SVG and JavaScript, adds no image asset, and therefore requires no image credit.
