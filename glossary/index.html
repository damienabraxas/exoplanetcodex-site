Glossary of Terms

A

Absorption line

Definition

A dark feature in a spectrum at the wavelength where atoms in the star's outer layers absorb light.

Plain English

Each element absorbs only certain colours of light. Where it does, the star's spectrum has a dark notch. Each element has its own set of notches, so the notches tell us which elements are there.

Units

Wavelength in ångströms (Å)

Example

Iron alone has thousands of lines in the Sun's visible spectrum. Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse (2021) chose 40 Fe I and 13 Fe II lines from them for their solar iron value.

In the Codex

Every abundance on this site comes from absorption lines. Method, section 01.

See also

Spectrum, Equivalent width (EW), Blend

Abundance ratio, [X/H] and [X/Fe]

Definition

[X/H] is a star's abundance of X minus the Sun's: [X/H] = A(X)star − A(X)Sun. [X/Fe] = [X/H] − [Fe/H] compares X with iron instead of hydrogen.

Plain English

These say how a star differs from the Sun. Zero means the same as the Sun. +0.3 means about twice as much. [X/Fe] removes the star's overall metal content, so it shows which elements are unusual.

Units

dex

Example

For the Sun every [X/H] and [X/Fe] is 0.00 by definition.

In the Codex

Ratios like these are step 14 of the process, after the abundances themselves. Method, section 01.

See also

Abundance, A(X), Metallicity, [Fe/H], Alpha enhancement, [α/Fe]

Abundance, A(X)

Definition

The number of atoms of element X for every 1012 hydrogen atoms, on a log scale: A(X) = log10(NX/NH) + 12. Hydrogen is 12.00 by definition.

Plain English

A way to say how much of an element a star has. Each step of 1 on the scale is a factor of ten. Iron at 7.46 means about 29 million iron atoms for every trillion hydrogen atoms.

Units

dex (logarithmic)

Example

The Sun: A(Fe) = 7.46, A(O) = 8.69, A(C) = 8.46 (Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse 2021).

In the Codex

This is the number every Codex result reports. Method, section 06.

See also

Abundance ratio, [X/H] and [X/Fe], dex, Metallicity, [Fe/H]

Alpha elements (α)

Definition

Elements made mostly in massive stars and released quickly when those stars explode: O, Ne, Mg, Si, S, Ar, Ca and Ti. The name comes from how their nuclei are built, by adding helium nuclei (alpha particles), but the group is defined by where the elements come from, not by that alone.

Plain English

A family of elements made in big, short-lived stars. Magnesium and silicon are among them, and they are the main rock-forming elements.

Why these

Counting helium nuclei alone would include almost everything up to iron. Carbon-12 is three helium nuclei, and chromium-52 and even iron-56 are whole numbers of them too. So astronomers group elements by how they behave over the galaxy's history instead. The alpha elements rise above iron in old stars and fall back to the Sun's ratio in young ones, because they came early and iron came later. Carbon does not follow that pattern, since much of it comes from smaller stars late in their lives. Chromium is made alongside iron and keeps the same ratio to iron in stars of every age, so it counts as an iron-peak element. Titanium is not strictly built this way, but it follows the alpha pattern in real stars, so it is included.

Units

None (an element group)

Example

Mg, Si, Ca and Ti are the alpha elements most often measured in Sun-like stars. Neon and argon cannot be measured in them at all: they leave no usable lines in the light from the star's surface.

In the Codex

Silicon has a published solar result on this site. Method, section 01.

See also

Alpha enhancement, [α/Fe], Iron-peak elements

Alpha enhancement, [α/Fe]

Definition

The average of [X/Fe] over a set of alpha elements. It traces how a star's birth gas was enriched over the history of the galaxy. Studies differ on which elements go into the average; the most common choice is Mg, Si, Ca and Ti.

Plain English

Early in the galaxy's life, massive stars made alpha elements quickly. Iron from slower kinds of supernova came later. Old stars therefore tend to have more alpha elements for their iron than young stars like the Sun.

Units

dex

Example

The Sun: [α/Fe] = 0.00 by definition.

In the Codex

Shown two ways, each worked out once all of its elements have passed review. Accepted [α/Fe] uses the usual four: Mg, Si, Ca and Ti. They are the alpha elements with the most clean lines in a Sun-like star. Codex [α/Fe] uses every alpha element we can measure: O, Mg, Si, S, Ca and Ti. Oxygen and sulfur are left out of the usual four because they have only a few lines, and oxygen's need large corrections. We measure both for the Sun anyway. Neon and argon are left out of both versions, because they leave no lines in the light from a Sun-like star. Method, section 01.

See also

Alpha elements (α), Abundance ratio, [X/H] and [X/Fe]

Atlas (solar flux atlas)

Definition

A very high-quality spectrum of the Sun's integrated light, published for other scientists to use. It is built from many observations and covers a wide wavelength range.

Plain English

A reference photograph of sunlight, spread out by colour in great detail. Teams around the world measure from the same atlas, so their results can be compared directly.

Units

Wavelength in Å; normalised flux

Example

The 1984 Kitt Peak solar atlas, and the IAG atlas from Göttingen (Reiners et al. 2016; Baker et al. 2020).

In the Codex

Each atlas is a separate holding. The IAG atlas also serves as an independent check on continuum placement. Method, section 02.

See also

Holding, Telluric absorption and correction, Continuum

ATLAS9

Definition

A widely used grid of one-dimensional model atmospheres for stars like the Sun, computed by Robert Kurucz and updated by Castelli & Kurucz (2003). Not to be confused with a solar atlas.

Plain English

A ready-made set of model star atmospheres, each one a table of temperature and pressure against depth.

Units

None (a model grid)

Example

ATLAS9 and MARCS are both 1D. Running the same lines on each shows how much the answer depends on the model.

In the Codex

One of two 1D model families we use. Swapping it for MARCS is one term in the error bar. Method, section 05.

See also

Model atmosphere, LTE (local thermodynamic equilibrium)

B

Band

Definition

A wavelength range of the spectrum that is analysed under its own rules. The Codex uses six: near-UV, visible, red-optical, near-infrared, H and K.

Plain English

We cut the spectrum into pieces because the problems change with colour. In the near-UV the lines crowd together. In the infrared the lines are sparse, but Earth's air absorbs strongly.

Units

Wavelength in Å

Example

The visible band runs from 3780 to 6910 Å. The H band runs from 15007 to 17494 Å.

In the Codex

Every band is measured on its own and never averaged with another. Method, section 02.

See also

Holding, Orchestrator, Spectrum

Barycentric correction

Definition

A shift applied to the wavelengths of an observation to remove the Earth's own motion around the Sun along the line of sight.

Plain English

The Earth moves, and that motion slightly shifts the colours we see. The correction removes the shift so that only the star's motion is left.

Units

km/s, or the matching shift in Å

Example

The standard HARPS data products already have this correction applied.

In the Codex

Checked during the audit of each holding. Method, section 01.

See also

Radial velocity (RV), Holding

Blend

Definition

Two or more absorption lines so close in wavelength that they overlap in the spectrum.

Plain English

Two notches that run into each other. If you measure one without allowing for the other, you get too much absorption and the wrong answer.

Units

None

Example

The forbidden oxygen line at 6300.304 Å sits only 0.032 Å from a nickel line at 6300.336 Å.

In the Codex

Blends are handled by calculating every known atomic and molecular line in the window together. Lines too blended to measure are left out, as published solar studies do. Method, section 04.

See also

Spectrum synthesis, Absorption line, Spectral resolution, R

C

C/O ratio

Definition

The number of carbon atoms divided by the number of oxygen atoms in a star.

Plain English

Carbon and oxygen compete for each other in the gas that forms planets. When oxygen wins, rocks are made of silicates, as on Earth. A star with much more carbon than usual could form very different planets.

Units

A ratio of numbers of atoms

Example

The Sun: C/O = 10(8.46 − 8.69) ≈ 0.59 (Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse 2021).

In the Codex

A derived ratio, worked out from the published carbon and oxygen results. Method, section 01.

See also

Abundance, A(X), Mg/Si and Fe/Si ratios

CHNOPS

Definition

The six elements that make up most of living matter: carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus and sulfur.

Plain English

The main ingredients of life as we know it. How much of each a star has hints at what its planets were built from.

Units

None (an element group)

Example

Carbon, nitrogen and oxygen have published solar results on this site.

In the Codex

Carbon, nitrogen and oxygen were among the first elements finished for the Sun. Method, section 01.

See also

C/O ratio, Abundance, A(X)

Continuum

Definition

The brightness a spectrum would have if there were no absorption lines. In a normalised spectrum it is set to 1.

Plain English

The level the notches are measured down from. If the level is wrong, every notch looks too deep or too shallow.

Units

Normalised flux (1.0)

Example

A line only 1% deep is changed noticeably by a continuum error of a tenth of a percent.

In the Codex

We set the continuum near each line by comparing the observed spectrum with our calculated one, and repeat on a second atlas to price the error. Method, section 03.

See also

Normalisation, Pseudo-continuum, Equivalent width (EW)

Correlated gf error

Definition

An error in oscillator strengths that is shared by every line measured in the same laboratory study, so it does not shrink when more lines are averaged.

Plain English

If a ruler is a millimetre short, measuring more things with it does not help. Lab gf values can be off in the same way: all lines from one study shift together.

Units

dex

Example

Lab gf values often combine an atomic lifetime with how the decay splits between lines. An error in the lifetime moves all lines from that level together.

In the Codex

For each gf source we record which part of its error is shared and which is per line. A source not yet reviewed is treated as fully shared. Method, section 07.

See also

Oscillator strength, log gf, Laboratory gf, Uncertainty budget

Curve of growth

Definition

The relation between a line's equivalent width and the abundance of the element. It has three parts: linear for weak lines, flat for saturated lines, and a damping part for very strong lines.

Plain English

For a weak line, twice the atoms gives twice the absorption. Once the line's centre is nearly black, adding atoms barely changes it. That flat stretch is why strong lines tell us less.

Units

log(equivalent width / wavelength) against log abundance

Example

Lines on the flat part depend strongly on microturbulence.

In the Codex

Turns a measured equivalent width into an abundance in the profile-fit route. Method, section 06.

See also

Equivalent width (EW), Saturated line, Microturbulence, ξ

D

Departure coefficient

Definition

The ratio of the true number of atoms in an energy level to the number LTE predicts. A value of 1 means LTE holds for that level.

Plain English

A correction factor for each energy level of an atom. It says how far the real atoms are from the simple assumption, layer by layer through the atmosphere.

Units

Dimensionless ratio

Example

Turbospectrum reads departure coefficients from an NLTE model atom and builds them into the calculated spectrum (Gerber et al. 2023).

In the Codex

Used by the NLTE treatments that put NLTE inside the calculation. Method, section 05.

See also

NLTE (non-local thermodynamic equilibrium), LTE (local thermodynamic equilibrium), Spectrum synthesis

dex

Definition

Short for decimal exponent. A difference of 1 dex is a factor of ten.

Plain English

The unit for log-scale numbers. Small differences are easier to think of as percentages: 0.01 dex is about 2%, and 0.1 dex is about 26%.

Units

Dimensionless (logarithmic)

Example

[Fe/H] = +0.30 means about twice as much iron as the Sun, because 100.30 ≈ 2.

In the Codex

All abundances and error bars on this site are in dex. Method, section 05.

See also

Abundance, A(X), Abundance ratio, [X/H] and [X/Fe]

E

Effective temperature, Teff

T-eff
Definition

The temperature of a perfect black body that gives off the same total power per unit area as the star.

Plain English

The star's surface temperature, in a precise sense. It sets which atoms are excited and so which lines are strong.

Units

Kelvin (K)

Example

The Sun: 5772 K (Heiter et al. 2015).

In the Codex

Fixed for the Sun before any line is measured. For the Sun it is known so well that its error-bar term comes out at zero. Method, section 07.

See also

Surface gravity, log g, Microturbulence, ξ, Model atmosphere

Engine

Definition

One complete way of turning spectra into abundances, including how departures from LTE are handled. The Codex has two. One applies published NLTE corrections to each line's result. The other puts NLTE inside the spectrum calculation itself.

Plain English

Two different calculators for the same question. If they agree, we trust the answer more. If they disagree, that tells us something about the physics.

Units

None

Example

Iron is measured with published correction tables and with Turbospectrum's built-in NLTE, and both results are shown.

In the Codex

Results from different engines and treatments are never averaged. Averaging would hide a disagreement that is real information. Method, section 05.

See also

NLTE (non-local thermodynamic equilibrium), Orchestrator, Spectrum synthesis

Equivalent width (EW)

Definition

The width of a completely dark strip with the same area as the absorption line.

Plain English

One number for how much light a line removes, whatever its shape. A strong line has a large equivalent width and a faint line a small one.

Units

Ångströms (Å) or milliångströms (mÅ); 1 Å = 1000 mÅ

Example

Many lines used for the Sun are a few tens of mÅ.

In the Codex

Measured in the profile-fit route, in the visible and red bands where lines are well separated. Method, section 06.

See also

Curve of growth, Absorption line, Continuum

F

Forbidden line

Definition

A line from a transition so unlikely that it is rarely seen in the laboratory, but appears in stars because the light passes through so much gas. It is written in brackets, as in [O I].

Plain English

A very weak kind of line. It forms under simple conditions, which makes it useful, but it is easily confused with any neighbour.

Units

None (a type of transition)

Example

[O I] 6300.304 Å is a classic solar oxygen line. A nickel line sits 0.032 Å away.

In the Codex

Used for oxygen, with its nickel neighbour included in the calculation. Method, section 04.

See also

Blend, Oscillator strength, log gf

FWHM (full width at half maximum)

Definition

The width of a line measured halfway between the continuum and the line's deepest point.

Plain English

A simple measure of how wide a line is. Narrow lines are less likely to run into their neighbours.

Units

Ångströms (Å)

Example

A line whose depth is 0.30 is measured for width where its depth is 0.15.

In the Codex

Part of describing a line's shape in the profile-fit route. Method, section 06.

See also

Equivalent width (EW), Spectral resolution, R, Voigt profile

G

Gaussian profile

Definition

A bell-shaped line profile. It describes broadening by random gas motions and by the instrument.

Plain English

The familiar bell curve. It drops off quickly away from the centre, so it has no long wings.

Units

Depth (0 to 1) and width σ (Å)

Example

Weak lines in a Sun-like star look close to Gaussian.

In the Codex

One part of the Voigt profile used in the profile-fit route. Method, section 06.

See also

Voigt profile, Lorentzian profile, Thermal broadening

H

HARPS

Definition

The High Accuracy Radial velocity Planet Searcher, a spectrograph on the ESO 3.6 m telescope at La Silla, Chile. Its resolution is about 115,000 and it covers 3780 to 6910 Å.

Plain English

A very stable instrument built to find planets by the wobble they cause in their star. Its spectra are also sharp enough to measure elements.

Units

Resolution R ≈ 115,000

Example

We use ten direct exposures of the Sun from HARPS, corrected for Earth's atmosphere with molecfit.

In the Codex

One of the solar holdings in the visible band. Method, section 02.

See also

Holding, Spectral resolution, R, Telluric absorption and correction

Holding

Definition

One instrument's observations of one star, as stored and prepared by the Codex.

Plain English

The set of spectra we have of a star from one instrument. The Sun has five: two versions of the Kitt Peak atlas, the IAG atlas, HARPS and CRIRES+.

Units

None

Example

The CRIRES+ holding is infrared sunlight reflected off the asteroid Vesta.

In the Codex

Each holding is corrected for Earth's atmosphere and normalised once, then frozen and used unchanged for every element. Method, section 02.

See also

Band, Atlas (solar flux atlas), Telluric absorption and correction

HST/STIS and HST/COS

Definition

Two ultraviolet spectrographs on the Hubble Space Telescope: the Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph and the Cosmic Origins Spectrograph.

Plain English

Earth's air blocks most ultraviolet light, so some lines can only be seen from space. These two instruments see them.

Units

None (instruments)

Example

Some carbon, nitrogen, oxygen and silicon lines lie only in the ultraviolet.

In the Codex

We hold Hubble spectra of planet hosts. They wait until the Sun is finished. Method, section 02.

See also

MAST, Band

Hyperfine structure

Definition

The splitting of one spectral line into several very close components, caused by the atomic nucleus. Different isotopes of an element split a line in a similar way.

Plain English

What looks like one line is really a cluster of tiny lines. Treat it as one and the shape is wrong, so the answer is wrong.

Units

Component separations in mÅ

Example

Manganese, cobalt, copper, scandium, vanadium, barium and europium lines need it.

In the Codex

The calculation includes every component, but the gf is fixed once for the whole physical line and the components are scaled to match. Method, section 04.

See also

Oscillator strength, log gf, Spectrum synthesis, Blend

I

Iron-peak elements

Definition

Elements near iron in the periodic table, such as V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co and Ni. Their nuclei are among the most tightly bound, so stars build up a lot of them.

Plain English

Iron and its neighbours. They are made largely in supernova explosions and end up in planet cores.

Units

None (an element group)

Example

Iron is the iron-peak element with the most usable lines.

In the Codex

Iron is always measured first, as the check that everything before it works. Method, section 01.

See also

Alpha elements (α), Metallicity, [Fe/H]

L

Laboratory gf

Definition

An oscillator strength measured in a laboratory, usually by timing how long an excited atom lives and measuring how its decay splits between lines.

Plain English

The line strength measured directly from real atoms, not calculated. It comes with a stated error, which is what lets us put a number on how much it can be trusted.

Units

dex (as log gf)

Example

Much of the solar iron work relies on laboratory gf from groups such as O'Brian et al. (1991).

In the Codex

Any line with a laboratory gf is added to the Reference grade. Method, section 04.

See also

Oscillator strength, log gf, Correlated gf error, Line grades: Reference, Codex and Deep

Line grades: Reference, Codex and Deep

Definition

Three sets of lines, chosen by how well each line's oscillator strength is known. Reference: the lines and gf values of the published solar analysis, plus any line with a laboratory gf. Codex: any line whose gf has a published uncertainty, up to 60% deep. Deep: the same standard for lines deeper than 60%.

Plain English

We first repeat the published work with the same lines, then widen the set. Every line in a grade has a gf error we can put a number on.

Units

None (a label)

Example

A line whose gf has no stated uncertainty can still be measured, but it does not reach a grade.

In the Codex

The grades are set before anything is measured. Method, section 04.

See also

Published line set, Oscillator strength, log gf, NIST accuracy grade

Literature check

Definition

A comparison of our result with the published solar values for the same element, each with the method it used.

Plain English

Before we trust our number, we compare it with what other careful teams found and how they found it.

Units

dex

Example

The visible band is the control. It has a known answer: iron at 7.46 (Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse 2021).

In the Codex

Published values are collected before measuring and compared after. The models are never adjusted to hit the published value. How far the method misses in the visible band goes into the error bar. Method, section 08.

See also

Publication gate, Published line set, Uncertainty budget

Lorentzian profile

Definition

A line profile with wide wings that fall off slowly away from the centre. It describes broadening by collisions in the gas.

Plain English

Like a bell curve with long skirts. Strong lines show these wings clearly.

Units

Half-width γ (Å)

Example

The broad wings of the sodium D lines are Lorentzian in shape.

In the Codex

One part of the Voigt profile used in the profile-fit route. Method, section 06.

See also

Voigt profile, Gaussian profile, Pressure broadening (damping)

LTE (local thermodynamic equilibrium)

Definition

The assumption that in each layer of the atmosphere, the atoms' energy levels and ionisation are set by that layer's temperature alone.

Plain English

The simplest way to calculate a spectrum. It treats every layer as if it were in a closed, evenly heated box.

Units

None (an approximation)

Example

Most strong lines in the Sun form well enough in LTE to be a useful starting point. Many still need an NLTE correction.

In the Codex

1D LTE is one of the treatments we run and report on its own. Method, section 05.

See also

NLTE (non-local thermodynamic equilibrium), Departure coefficient, Model atmosphere

M

MAST

Definition

The Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes, NASA's public archive of Hubble and other space-telescope data.

Plain English

The library where Hubble's observations are kept for anyone to download.

Units

None (an archive)

Example

Our Hubble spectra of planet hosts come from MAST.

In the Codex

The source of our Hubble holdings. Method, section 02.

See also

HST/STIS and HST/COS, Holding

Median over lines

Definition

The middle value of the line-by-line abundances, once they are put in order.

Plain English

Each line gives its own answer. We take the middle one, so a single odd line cannot pull the result far.

Units

dex

Example

Five lines giving 7.40, 7.44, 7.46, 7.47 and 7.90 have a median of 7.46. Their average would be 7.53.

In the Codex

A result is the median over its lines. When the published analysis we are repeating used a plain average, we use the same. Method, section 06.

See also

Abundance, A(X), Statistical and systematic uncertainty

Metallicity, [Fe/H]

Definition

A star's iron abundance compared with the Sun's: [Fe/H] = A(Fe)star − A(Fe)Sun. It is used as a stand-in for all elements heavier than helium.

Plain English

How metal-rich a star is compared with the Sun. Stars with more metals are more likely to host giant planets.

Units

dex

Example

The Sun: [Fe/H] = 0.00 by definition.

In the Codex

Iron is always measured first. For the Sun its metal-content error term comes out at zero. Method, section 07.

See also

Abundance ratio, [X/H] and [X/Fe], Abundance, A(X), Iron-peak elements

Mg/Si and Fe/Si ratios

Definition

The number of magnesium or iron atoms divided by the number of silicon atoms in a star.

Plain English

Rocky planets are built mostly from magnesium, silicon, iron and oxygen. Mg/Si shapes which minerals form the mantle. Fe/Si hints at how big the iron core is.

Units

Ratios of numbers of atoms

Example

The Sun: Mg/Si = 10(7.55 − 7.51) ≈ 1.10 and Fe/Si = 10(7.46 − 7.51) ≈ 0.89 (Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse 2021).

In the Codex

Derived ratios, worked out after the elements behind them are published. Method, section 01.

See also

C/O ratio, Alpha elements (α)

Microturbulence, ξ

xi, “ksee”
Definition

A speed that stands in for small-scale gas motions in the atmosphere that are not included in a 1D model. It broadens the absorption of each atom.

Plain English

Small swirls of gas spread a line's absorption over a wider range of colours. That matters most for strong lines, which are otherwise nearly black at their centre.

Units

km/s

Example

The Sun: ξ = 1.0 km/s (Jofré et al. 2014).

In the Codex

One of the stellar parameters fixed before measuring. Unlike temperature and gravity, its error term for the Sun is not zero, so we measure it by raising and lowering ξ. Method, section 07.

See also

Saturated line, Curve of growth, Model atmosphere

Model atmosphere

Definition

A table of temperature, pressure and density against depth through a star's outer layers. A 1D model uses flat layers. A full 3D model simulates the rising and sinking gas. A ⟨3D⟩ model is a 3D simulation averaged back into layers.

Plain English

A recipe for what the star's outer layers look like, so we can predict how each line should form.

Units

None (a model)

Example

We use two 1D families, MARCS (Gustafsson et al. 2008) and ATLAS9 (Castelli & Kurucz 2003), and ⟨3D⟩ models from the Stagger grid (Magic et al. 2013).

In the Codex

Each atmosphere gives its own result. Swapping MARCS for ATLAS9 is one term in the error bar. Method, section 05.

See also

ATLAS9, LTE (local thermodynamic equilibrium), NLTE (non-local thermodynamic equilibrium)

N

NIST accuracy grade

Definition

A letter grade given by the US National Institute of Standards and Technology to an atomic transition probability, based on its estimated uncertainty. The scale runs AAA, AA, A+, A, B+, B, C+, C, D+, D and E.

Plain English

A report card for a line's strength. Grade A means the value is good to a few percent. Grade C means within about 25%.

Units

Letter grade; A is within 3%, B within 10%, C within 25%, D within 50%

Example

A line with a NIST grade of A to C can join the Codex grade. Grades D and E cannot.

In the Codex

Used to decide which lines reach a grade. Method, section 04.

See also

Oscillator strength, log gf, Line grades: Reference, Codex and Deep, Laboratory gf

NLTE (non-local thermodynamic equilibrium)

Definition

Any calculation that does not assume LTE. The numbers of atoms in each energy level are worked out from all the processes that fill and empty them, including light coming up from deeper layers.

Plain English

In a star's thin outer layers, light from below also excites the atoms. Allowing for this changes how deep a line is predicted to be.

Units

dex (as a correction to an abundance)

Example

The oxygen lines near 7774 Å are a well-known case where NLTE matters a great deal.

In the Codex

We include NLTE two ways: published correction tables for each line, and departure coefficients inside the calculation. Each is its own result. Method, section 05.

See also

LTE (local thermodynamic equilibrium), Departure coefficient, Engine

Normalisation

Definition

Dividing a spectrum by its continuum so that the continuum is at 1. Line depths are then fractions of the continuum.

Plain English

Levelling the spectrum so every notch is measured from the same height.

Units

None (flux becomes a fraction)

Example

Near each line we compare the observed spectrum with our calculated one and set the level from that comparison. Points that disagree badly, such as an unlisted line, are clipped out first.

In the Codex

Done once per holding with Earth's atmosphere removed, then frozen for every element. Method, section 03.

See also

Continuum, Pseudo-continuum, Telluric absorption and correction

O

Orchestrator

Definition

The program that runs the whole process for one star and one element from a single command. It works through every combination of band, holding and treatment, and reports the status of each one.

Plain English

One command per element. The program does the measuring and tells us, for every combination, whether it is done, held back and why, or not run yet.

Units

None

Example

For the Sun, one command per element measures every band, instrument and treatment and builds each error bar.

In the Codex

People approve line removals and publication; the orchestrator does the rest. Method, section 01 and the Science Architecture.

See also

Engine, Band, Publication gate

Oscillator strength, log gf

Definition

A measure of how strongly an atom absorbs at one line, written as the log of g × f: the statistical weight of the lower level times the oscillator strength.

Plain English

How good an atom is at absorbing that particular colour. A wrong value gives a wrong abundance, however good the spectrum is.

Units

Dimensionless (logarithmic)

Example

Fe I 5247.05 Å has log gf = −4.961 in the published solar iron set (Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse 2021).

In the Codex

Fixed once for each physical line from a single master table. A line with no entry stops the run. Method, section 04.

See also

Laboratory gf, NIST accuracy grade, Correlated gf error

P

Pressure broadening (damping)

Definition

Widening of a line by collisions between the absorbing atom and other particles in the gas. It gives a line Lorentzian wings.

Plain English

Atoms that bump into neighbours absorb a slightly wider range of colours. In dense gas, strong lines get wide wings.

Units

Å; described by damping constants

Example

The sodium D lines in the Sun have wings that stretch for several ångströms.

In the Codex

Part of every calculated line profile. Method, section 06.

See also

Lorentzian profile, Voigt profile, Saturated line

Pseudo-continuum

Definition

The highest level a spectrum reaches where lines cover every wavelength, so the true continuum is never seen.

Plain English

In the near-UV the notches overlap everywhere. The best we can do is a level close to, but below, the true one.

Units

Normalised flux

Example

Below about 3780 Å no solar line stands alone.

In the Codex

Its uncertainty is priced as a separate term, for near-UV results only. Method, section 03.

See also

Continuum, Normalisation, Band

Publication gate

Definition

The checks a result must pass before it is published: every error-bar term present, a comparison with the literature, and a person's approval.

Plain English

Being computed is not enough. A result goes on the site only when it has a complete error bar and someone has reviewed it.

Units

None

Example

A result missing any error-bar term is held back and no error bar is published for it. A held result is one we know we still owe, with the reason recorded.

In the Codex

Every proposed line removal and every publication is approved by a person. Method, section 08.

See also

Literature check, Uncertainty budget, Superseded result

Published line set

Definition

The lines, and their gf values, that a published solar analysis used for an element.

Plain English

The exact recipe another team used. We repeat it first, so that any difference from their answer comes from our spectra or our method, and we can find it.

Units

None

Example

For iron, Asplund, Amarsi & Grevesse (2021) used 40 Fe I and 13 Fe II lines. For other elements they cite the paper the lines come from.

In the Codex

The published set, plus any line with a laboratory gf, makes the Reference grade. Method, section 04.

See also

Line grades: Reference, Codex and Deep, Literature check, Oscillator strength, log gf

R

Radial velocity (RV)

Definition

A star's speed toward or away from us, measured from the shift of its spectral lines.

Plain English

A star moving away looks slightly redder, and one moving toward us slightly bluer. The lines must be shifted back to their rest wavelengths before measuring.

Units

km/s

Example

At 5900 Å, a speed of 30 km/s shifts a line by about 0.6 Å.

In the Codex

Each holding is checked to serve every line at its rest wavelength. Method, section 02.

See also

Barycentric correction, Holding

Rotational broadening, v sin i

Definition

Widening of lines by the star's spin. One edge of the star moves toward us and the other away.

Plain English

A spinning star smears its lines. Slow spinners like the Sun have sharp lines.

Units

km/s

Example

The Sun: v sin i = 1.8 km/s.

In the Codex

Part of the line broadening used in every calculated spectrum. Method, section 06.

See also

Thermal broadening, Pressure broadening (damping)

S

s-process and r-process elements

Definition

Elements heavier than iron made by capturing neutrons. The slow (s) process works inside aging giant stars. The rapid (r) process needs extreme events such as merging neutron stars.

Plain English

Two very different ways nature builds heavy elements. Barium and yttrium come mostly from the slow route. Europium comes mostly from the rapid one.

Units

None (element groups)

Example

Ba II, Y II and Eu II lines are visible in Sun-like stars.

In the Codex

Heavy elements will run through the same process after the lighter ones. Method, section 01.

See also

Hyperfine structure, Abundance, A(X)

Saturated line

Definition

A line so strong that its centre is nearly black, so its depth barely changes when the abundance changes.

Plain English

Once the notch is almost fully dark, adding atoms hardly changes it. The line then tells us more about gas motions than about how many atoms there are.

Units

None

Example

The sodium D lines in the Sun are saturated.

In the Codex

A saturated line that the published solar analyses did not use is proposed for removal, and a person approves each one. Method, section 04.

See also

Curve of growth, Microturbulence, ξ, Line grades: Reference, Codex and Deep

Signal-to-noise ratio, S/N

Definition

The strength of the signal divided by the size of the random noise in a spectrum.

Plain English

How clean the spectrum is. With high S/N, faint notches stand out clearly from the noise.

Units

Dimensionless ratio

Example

Combining N similar exposures raises S/N by about √N.

In the Codex

Noise in the spectrum feeds the statistical part of each error bar. Method, section 07.

See also

Statistical and systematic uncertainty, Spectrum

Spectral resolution, R

Definition

R = λ/Δλ, where Δλ is the smallest wavelength gap the instrument can separate at wavelength λ.

Plain English

How fine the detail is. At higher R, two lines close together appear as two lines, not one smudge.

Units

Dimensionless ratio

Example

HARPS has R ≈ 115,000, so at 6000 Å it separates features about 0.05 Å apart. The 2010 thesis used ELODIE, at R ≈ 42,000.

In the Codex

Every holding we use has high enough resolution to separate most solar lines. Method, section 02.

See also

HARPS, Blend, FWHM (full width at half maximum)

Spectral type

Definition

A classification of stars by surface temperature and the pattern of their lines, from hottest to coolest: O, B, A, F, G, K, M.

Plain English

A quick label for what kind of star it is. The Sun is a G star. Slightly cooler, orange stars are K stars.

Units

None (a classification)

Example

The Sun: G2V. 55 Cancri A, a planet host we hold spectra for, is a K star.

In the Codex

The method is proven on the Sun before other stars. Method, section 02.

See also

Effective temperature, Teff, Surface gravity, log g

Spectrum

Definition

The light from an object spread out by wavelength, showing how much light arrives at each wavelength.

Plain English

A star's light spread into a rainbow, in great detail. The dark notches in it are the star's chemical fingerprint.

Units

Wavelength in Å; normalised flux from 0 to 1

Example

The solar spectra we use run from 3000 Å in the near-UV to 24846 Å in the infrared.

In the Codex

Every result starts from a spectrum that has been corrected and normalised. Method, section 01.

See also

Absorption line, Continuum, Spectral resolution, R

Spectrum synthesis

Definition

Calculating the spectrum a model atmosphere would produce for a given set of abundances, including every known atomic and molecular line in the window.

Plain English

We compute what the spectrum should look like, then change the amount of the element until the computed spectrum matches the real one.

Units

None (a method)

Example

We use Turbospectrum (Plez 2012) through iSpec (Blanco-Cuaresma et al. 2014).

In the Codex

The main route. In the near-UV it is the only route allowed, because no line there stands alone. Method, section 06.

See also

Blend, Model atmosphere, Engine

Statistical and systematic uncertainty

Definition

Statistical uncertainty comes from the scatter between lines. Systematic uncertainty is how far the answer moves when a choice we had to make is changed.

Plain English

Statistical error shrinks when more lines are used. Systematic error does not, because every line shares the same choice.

Units

dex

Example

With several lines, the statistical error is the scatter divided by the square root of the number of lines.

In the Codex

Both parts go into every published error bar. Method, section 07.

See also

Uncertainty budget, Correlated gf error, Median over lines

Superseded result

Definition

A published result that has been replaced by a newer measurement of the same thing.

Plain English

When we redo a measurement, for example after a fix to the atomic data, the new result replaces the old one. The old one is not deleted.

Units

None

Example

A superseded result is moved to an archive with the record of why it was replaced.

In the Codex

Only current results are shown, and every earlier number can still be traced. Method, section 08.

See also

Publication gate, Literature check

Surface gravity, log g

Definition

The base-10 logarithm of the gravity at the star's surface, in cgs units (cm/s²). It sets the pressure in the atmosphere.

Plain English

How strongly the star pulls on its outer layers. Higher gravity means denser gas, which changes line widths and how many atoms are ionised.

Units

log10(cm/s²)

Example

The Sun: log g = 4.44 (Heiter et al. 2015).

In the Codex

Fixed before measuring. For the Sun its error-bar term comes out at zero. Method, section 07.

See also

Effective temperature, Teff, Model atmosphere

T

Telluric absorption and correction

Definition

Telluric absorption is absorption of starlight by Earth's own atmosphere, mostly water vapour and oxygen. Telluric correction removes it from the spectrum.

Plain English

Before starlight reaches a telescope on the ground, Earth's air takes its own bites out of it. Those bites land on top of the star's lines and must be removed first.

Units

None

Example

For HARPS and CRIRES+ we model the air on the night of each observation with molecfit (Smette et al. 2015).

In the Codex

Every spectrum taken from the ground is corrected. Only a telescope in space escapes this. Method, section 03.

See also

Holding, Atlas (solar flux atlas), Normalisation

Thermal broadening

Definition

Widening of a line by the random motion of the absorbing atoms, which Doppler-shifts each atom's absorption slightly.

Plain English

Hot atoms move fast in all directions. Each one sees the light at a slightly different colour, which spreads the line.

Units

Å; gives a Gaussian shape

Example

Lighter atoms move faster at the same temperature, so their lines are wider.

In the Codex

Part of every calculated line profile. Method, section 06.

See also

Gaussian profile, Microturbulence, ξ

U

Uncertainty budget

Definition

The list of every source of error in a result, each measured separately, then added in quadrature: the square root of the sum of squares.

Plain English

A full account of what could move the answer. For each choice we re-run the same lines with that choice changed and record how far the answer moves.

Units

dex

Example

Terms include the gf values, temperature, gravity, metal content, microturbulence, continuum, fit window, Earth's atmosphere, NLTE, model atmosphere, hyperfine structure, blends and a method check.

In the Codex

Every result carries every term. If one is missing, the result is held back. Method, section 07.

See also

Statistical and systematic uncertainty, Correlated gf error, Publication gate

V

VALD

Definition

The Vienna Atomic Line Database, a widely used collection of atomic and molecular line data: wavelengths, energy levels, oscillator strengths and broadening constants (Ryabchikova et al. 2015).

Plain English

A large public catalogue of spectral lines. It tells us where lines are and roughly how strong, but not every value in it has a measured error.

Units

None (a database)

Example

We extract solar line lists from VALD3, with hyperfine components included.

In the Codex

Supplies the neighbouring lines in each synthesis window. The gf of each line we measure comes from the master table instead. Method, section 04.

Oscillator strength, log gf, Spectrum synthesis, Blend

Voigt profile

Definition

A line profile that combines a Gaussian core with Lorentzian wings: the mathematical convolution of the two.

Plain English

The realistic shape of a stellar line: bell-shaped in the middle, with longer wings for strong lines.

Units

Centre (Å), Gaussian width and Lorentzian width (Å)

Example

Fitting a strong line with only a Gaussian misses its wings and underestimates its equivalent width.

In the Codex

The shape fitted in the profile-fit route. Method, section 06.

Gaussian profile, Lorentzian profile, Equivalent width (EW)
